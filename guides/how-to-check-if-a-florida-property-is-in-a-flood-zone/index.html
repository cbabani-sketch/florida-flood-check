Florida Flood Check

How to Check if a Florida Property Is in a Flood Zone (Free, 5 Minutes)

Free ways to look up the FEMA flood zone for any Florida address, what to check besides the zone, and the questions to ask before you buy, sell or insure.

1. Look up the FEMA flood map (free)

Go to FEMA's Flood Map Service Center (msc.fema.gov), type the address, and open the map. The zone label on the property (AE, VE, X and so on) is its official flood zone. Many Florida counties also show the zone on their property appraiser or GIS map.

2. Check the ground elevation

The zone is only part of the story. A home a few feet above the street can stay dry while the neighbor floods. An Elevation Certificate, made by a licensed surveyor, compares the lowest floor to the expected flood level; ask the seller if one exists.

3. Ask about history

  • Has the property ever flooded or had water in the house?
  • Were there past flood insurance claims or FEMA assistance?
  • What is the current flood insurance premium?

4. Price the insurance before you commit

Get a flood quote from an agent (NFIP or private) during your inspection period, not after closing. In high-risk zones, lenders on federally backed mortgages require flood insurance.

Questions

Is flood insurance required in Florida?

If the home is in a high-risk zone (A or V) and has a federally backed mortgage, yes. Outside those zones it is optional but often recommended in Florida.

Is the FEMA flood map free?

Yes. FEMA's Flood Map Service Center is free for anyone to use.
